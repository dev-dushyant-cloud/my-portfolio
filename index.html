Volume 01 — Signal

Software with a< br/>center of gravity.

Most sites are a stack of flat rectangles. I treat a product like a room: things sit at different distances, carry different weight, and respond when you move through them. That framing is not decoration — it forces honest decisions about hierarchy, latency and state, because everything has to remain legible in motion.

My work usually starts where the design file ends: the data model, the request lifecycle, the 200ms that decides whether an interaction feels physical or fictional.

02 / SELF

The operator

Write the API first. Then the screen. Then the test that proves the screen was right about the API.

I grew up on a dial-up connection in Rajasthan and never quite lost the habit of treating every kilobyte as borrowed. That became a working style: measure before you optimise, delete before you abstract, and assume the user is on a mid-range Android in a train tunnel.

Today I work as a full-stack developer across the whole vertical — React and Next.js on the surface, Node and Go beneath, PostgreSQL and Redis holding the truth, AWS and edge runtimes carrying it. I care most about the seam: the moment a click becomes a request becomes a row becomes a re-render. Get that smooth and everything downstream feels expensive.

I've been the only engineer on a seed-stage product, and I've been one of eleven on a platform team. Both teach something the other can't. Solo work teaches ruthless scoping; teams teach contract design, migration etiquette, and how to leave a codebase warmer than you found it.

Away from the editor: mechanical keyboards (currently a 65% with tactiles I regret owning), long night drives on the Jaipur–Delhi stretch, and a stubborn habit of rebuilding every tool I use more than twice.

06 / FLOW

How a build runs

Five phases, roughly two-week beats. The line fills as you descend.

Phase 01Interrogate

Two weeks of questions before code. Who actually uses this, on what device, in what conditions? I map the real workflow — including the spreadsheet everyone secretly still relies on — and name the one metric the product has to move. Everything else is negotiable and I say so early.

Phase 02Frame

Schema and contract first. Entities, relationships, access rules, and the typed API surface between layers, committed as a walking skeleton that deploys on day three. Ambiguity gets killed here, where it's cheap.

Phase 03Inhabit

Build in vertical slices, each one usable end-to-end. Interface, service and persistence land together so feedback is real rather than theoretical. Weekly preview URLs, no staging backlog, no “wait for the sprint demo”.

Phase 04Harden

Accessibility pass, performance budget enforced in CI, error budgets agreed, load tested at three times expected traffic. Backups restored for real. This phase is unglamorous and it is why clients call me back.

Phase 05Hand off & tend

Documented locally runnable setup, traced in production, and a team that can ship without me. I stay on retainer for the first quarter because the second release is where the architecture gets its honest review.
